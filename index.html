The Kinetic Energy Increase Paradox

When the skater pulls weights inward, $I$ drops so $\omega$ spikes up. Watch the rotational kinetic energy: $$K_{rot} = \frac{L^2}{2I}$$ Because $L$ is constant and $I$ decreased, $K_{rot}$ actually increases!

Where does this energy come from? The skater's muscles do positive internal work against the centrifugal tendency while pulling the weights inward!

Pure Rolling Motion on an Inclined Plane

The Great Race: Ring, Disc, Solid Sphere & Hollow Sphere down an incline of angle $\theta$

🏆 Solid Sphere Finishes First!
Incline Angle ($\theta$): 25°
Pure Rolling Acceleration: $a = \frac{g\sin\theta}{1 + k^2/R^2}$
1. Solid Sphere ($k^2/R^2 = 2/5$) 0.714 g sinθ
2. Solid Disc ($k^2/R^2 = 1/2$) 0.667 g sinθ
3. Hollow Sphere ($k^2/R^2 = 2/3$) 0.600 g sinθ
4. Ring / Shell ($k^2/R^2 = 1$) 0.500 g sinθ

Smaller $k^2/R^2$ (radius of gyration ratio) → smaller fraction of potential energy goes to spin → faster linear acceleration! Mass and radius do NOT matter!

Translational vs. Rotational Dynamics Analogies

Translational Motion Rotational Motion Connecting Bridge
Displacement $x$ Angular Pos $\theta$ $s = r\theta$
Velocity $v = \frac{dx}{dt}$ Angular Velocity $\omega = \frac{d\theta}{dt}$ $v = r\omega$
Acceleration $a = \frac{dv}{dt}$ Angular Accel $\alpha = \frac{d\omega}{dt}$ $a_t = r\alpha,\; a_c = r\omega^2$
Mass $m$ (Inertia) Moment of Inertia $I$ $I = \sum m_i r_i^2 = \int r^2 dm$
Force $\vec{F} = m\vec{a}$ Torque $\vec{\tau} = I\vec{\alpha}$ $\vec{\tau} = \vec{r} \times \vec{F}$
Linear Momentum $\vec{p} = m\vec{v}$ Angular Momentum $\vec{L} = I\vec{\omega}$ $\vec{L} = \vec{r} \times \vec{p}$
